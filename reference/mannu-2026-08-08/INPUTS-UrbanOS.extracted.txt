A note specific to Dwarka Expressway
For projects along the Dwarka Expressway (including sectors under Gurugram and adjoining planning areas), the planning process should additionally verify jurisdiction-specific inputs before design begins, such as:
	•	Applicable planning authority and sector plan.
	•	Current zoning and land-use permissions.
	•	CLU status (where relevant).
	•	FAR, density, height, and setback controls applicable to the site.
	•	Proposed and existing road network, including service roads and sector roads.
	•	Utility availability and planned infrastructure.
	•	Any corridor-specific restrictions, reservations, or infrastructure proposals that affect the parcel.

This workflow mirrors how experienced development firms operate: verify legal ownership → establish accurate site data → confirm regulatory constraints → understand market and financial objectives → then produce a compliance-first master plan. For UrbanOS, these inputs can become a structured intake system that blocks layout generation until all mandatory information has been supplied or verified, ensuring that every master plan is based on validated data rather than assumptions.
  For UrbanOS, you should think of every input in three categories:
	•	Permanent Database → Data that UrbanOS continuously maintains and updates for every city/sector.
	•	Project-Specific User Inputs → Data unique to the client's site or project.
	•	AI-Derived Data → Information generated automatically after combining the database with user inputs.
This separation is one of the biggest competitive advantages you can build. Commercial software like AutoCAD or Civil 3D provides design tools, but it does not maintain a planning intelligence database that understands regulations, land use, feasibility, and market context.

	•	Land use and zoning
	•	DCR and building bye-laws
	•	Road network
	•	Administrative boundaries
	•	Satellite imagery

Recommendation for UrbanOS Architecture
Instead of thinking of these as simple "inputs," organize your platform into four foundational knowledge layers:
	•	Regulatory Intelligence Layer – planning regulations, zoning, DCRs, byelaws, approval workflows, and policies.
	•	Spatial Intelligence Layer – GIS, satellite imagery, cadastral data, terrain, utilities, and environmental constraints.
	•	Market Intelligence Layer – pricing, demand, demographics, competitors, infrastructure, and economic indicators.
	•	Project Intelligence Layer – all client-specific documents, surveys, financial assumptions, and project objectives.
This architecture will make UrbanOS scalable beyond Dwarka Expressway to any city or region by replacing only the first three regional knowledge layers while keeping the Project Intelligence Layer universal.

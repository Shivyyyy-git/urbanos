## The design language these ten agree on (the taste extract)

1. **Warm, material palettes** — beige/terracotta/layered greens; never cold CAD primaries.
2. **Furniture and fixtures always present at unit scale** — usability must be *visible* (directly answers the 2.5/10 "no beds, no wardrobes, untested usability" finding).
3. **The sheet is a designed object** — title, sidebar schedules, legend, insets composed like a poster, not a drawing dumped on white.
4. **Trees drawn individually**, greens layered; landscape is the hero at site scale, with muted grey context making the site glow.
5. **Metric, numbered room schedules** shown proudly (picks 9, 10) — precision as a design feature, not a burden.
6. **Two genres coexist and we need both**: the professional-beautiful hybrid (pick 10) for the working sheet, and the photoreal/editorial warmth (picks 2, 6, 9) for the client-facing layer.
7. **Comparison is a first-class artifact** (pick 5): options presented as A/B with reasoned, icon-annotated verdicts.

## Immediate product consequences

- The **≥2-options output** adopts pick 5's format: side-by-side plans, each with reasoned verdict badges (flow, privacy, waste, conflicts — plus our compliance/actionability line). To Sol for the completeness-standard gates.
- The **unit sheet** targets pick 10's hybrid: dimensioned metric plan + grid + section + palette on one composed board; the editorial furniture-plan genre (9) is the client-facing variant of the same model.
- The **masterplan sheet** targets: muted context, individual trees, landscape spine hero, unit-mix zones legible at scale (picks 1, 4, 7, 8), with board composition per pick 3.
- Area statements: carpet / built-up / super always separated (pick 6 is the anti-pattern) [locked, per Mannu].

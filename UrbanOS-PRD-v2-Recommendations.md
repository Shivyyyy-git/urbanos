# UrbanOS — PRD v2 Recommendations

*Prepared 19 July 2026 · Companion to `UrbanOS PRD.pdf` and the MVP prototype in this folder.*
*Grounded in web research on Autodesk Forma, TestFit, ArchiStar, Giraffe, Deepblocks, Digital Blue Foam, SoftTech AutoDCR/PreDCR, Zoneity, and Indian regulatory/case-law context; full research briefs in the appendices.*

## 1. Sharpest value proposition

**"Know what any Indian plot can legally become — and the most you should pay for it — in minutes: sanction-grade FSI, compliant massing, and residual land value, with every rule cited to its bylaw clause."**

Rationale: the research found no India-focused generative site-design + feasibility product; the proven playbook (Deepblocks' parcel zoning data at $555–680/mo/city, TestFit's Site Solver from ~$8,000/yr, ArchiStar's due-diligence reports) exists only on US/AU data. In India, approval risk — not design cost — kills deals, and the answer today is a ₹3–10L, 4–8-week consultant engagement. The moat is the machine-readable, versioned, clause-citable Indian DCR/bylaw corpus — not the AI, which is commoditized (Forma ships generative massing at ~$185/mo). Accordingly, reposition the MVP as a **land-feasibility decision engine** for a specific buyer, and move "the operating system for all development" to the long-term vision section.

## 2. The wedge

**Who:** mid-market residential developers (CREDAI tier-1/2 members — CREDAI represents ~13,000 developers across ~230 chapters) and the boutique architects and DTCP-licensed consultants who serve them.

**What:** the land-acquisition go/no-go moment — the ₹3–25L, 4–12-week pre-feasibility cycle that precedes every serious land deal (and runs on 5–10 parcels for every 1 bought). User inputs a plot (map polygon, khasra number via Bhulekh/Bhu Naksha, or DXF) and gets in under five minutes: the permissible envelope (FSI/FAR including premium/purchasable FSI and TDR options, setbacks, ground coverage, height, parking — with clause-level citations), 2–3 compliant massing concepts with unit mix, a RERA-consistent saleable-area statement, and a cost-revenue model outputting **residual land value** — "the most I should pay for this land." The 10x moment: a broker sends a plot pin on WhatsApp at 11am; by 11:10 the developer has three bylaw-checked options and a shareable report — before the site visit is scheduled, and *before* the MoU is signed, which is exactly when developers pay for certainty.

**Where:** Gurugram/NCR first (DTCP Haryana/GMDA — the PRD's own Dwarka Expressway example), then Maharashtra, where the unified UDCPR means one rulebook unlocks hundreds of ULBs.

**Why now, and why the whitespace is real:**
- Global vendors won't digitize 35 states' DCRs; TestFit/Forma/ArchiStar have zero incentive to build Indian rule arithmetic (road-width-dependent FAR, purchasable premium FSI/TDR, saleable-area loading).
- SoftTech AutoDCR (500+ ULBs; ₹128.3 Cr FY26 revenue, +37% YoY, ₹232 Cr order book) automates the *government's* side of the counter — scrutinizing finished CAD drawings — not the developer's upstream go/no-go question. Ride it, don't fight it: make outputs PreDCR-layer-compatible so "generated by UrbanOS" means "sails through scrutiny."
- Data tailwinds: AMRUT GIS master plans, the 2021 geospatial deregulation, DILRMP land-record digitization, the National Geospatial Mission.
- The pattern from every winner (TestFit's decade on multifamily unit counts; Spacemaker's single site-optioning wedge worth a $240M Autodesk acquisition): one typology, one buyer, one decision moment, one artifact — with bounded-geography data depth as the moat.
- **UrbanOS is not first in India**: Zoneity (zoneity.app) already markets AI zoning intelligence across six Indian cities — so win on **depth** (amendment tracking, authority-specific quirks, sanctioned precedents) in one corridor rather than breadth.

## 4. Revised MVP scope

One loop, one asset class (residential group housing + plotted), two geographies (Gurugram/NCR first, Maharashtra via UDCPR second):

- **Module 1 — Project Intake** *(upgraded)*: draw-polygon on map, khasra lookup, DXF/KML upload, road-width capture; manual entry stays as fallback.
- **Module 2 — Parametric Envelope & Massing Engine** *(replaces "AI Planning Assistant")*: deterministic FSI/FAR envelope → 2–3 compliant massing concepts with unit mix (the MVP prototype's Yield-Max / Balanced / Green archetypes), generate-then-edit parameters, side-by-side scenarios. No walkthroughs.
- **Module 3 — Pre-scrutiny Engine** *(renamed, deepened — the moat)*: the ~20 envelope-determining rules per city with clause citations, version/date stamps, confidence, remediation; PreDCR-compatible framing ("maximize first-pass approval odds"). One city done deeply beats four done shallowly.
- **Module 4 — Feasibility Engine** *(kept — the buyer's module)*: cost/revenue/ROI, budget fit, sensitivity, viability score, RERA-consistent saleable-area statement, premium-FSI/TDR optimizer, **residual land value as the headline output**; every rate user-editable (developers always override defaults). License micro-market price data (Liases Foras/Propstack; public RERA filings as the free wedge) rather than rebuilding it.
- **Module 5 — Deliverables** *(upgraded from "Report Generator")*: branded PDF + share link, DXF/DWG + Excel exports, saved versions, comparison page, disclaimer block. This is the growth channel.
- **Beneath everything: the DCR data pipeline** — digitization, amendment tracking, versioning, legal review of sources, human verification ops, costed as COGS.

**Cut from MVP:** 3D walkthroughs, construction intelligence, sustainability engine, government/smart-city buyers, homeowners, mixed-use/industrial typologies, fire-NOC/EC adjudication.

## 5. Business model & first GTM motion

**Who pays first (ranked):** 1) mid-market developers (₹50–500 Cr GDV — they fund feasibility per-deal, promoter-led fast decisions); 2) planning/DCR consultancies & architects (10–50 feasibilities/yr — the volume users and the channel); 3) NBFC/HFC land-lending desks (year-2 underwriting expansion). Governments Phase 2+ only.

**Pricing (per verdict, not per seat — US seat anchors don't transplant into a market with ~56–58% software piracy):**
- **Per-report credits:** free instant "site snapshot" (FSI, setbacks, envelope) as the lead magnet → ₹25,000–₹75,000 per full feasibility + pre-scrutiny report; ₹1.5–3L for a multi-option masterplan pack. Anchored against ₹3–10L / 4–8-week consultant engagements.
- **Seat SaaS for consultancies/architects:** ₹60,000–₹1,00,000/seat/yr (Indian SMEs have proven ~₹70K/yr WTP — Powerplay prices from ~₹72K; Forma is ~₹1.25L equivalent).
- **Project license for large developers:** ₹5–15L per project (~0.05% of a ₹300 Cr GDV), feasibility through RERA-lifecycle monitoring — converting RERA's quarterly-filing cadence into per-project recurring revenue.
- Avoid success-fees on land-value uplift: unenforceable, dispute-prone.

**First motion — geography beats persona:** win Gurugram/NCR in depth before city two (Zoneity is six-cities-shallow). 5–10 lighthouse mid-size NCR developers via CREDAI NCR; side-by-side validation vs their consultants' feasibilities on live parcels; publish "6 weeks → 48 hours" case studies. Channel: DTCP-licensed consultants and RERA-filing firms as white-label resellers — they touch every project at registration. Free per-city FSI calculators to own "FSI Gurgaon"-class search queries; free tier for CoA architects/colleges (distribution, not revenue). Moat from day 1: versioned bylaw packs + anonymized ₹/sqft benchmark data from user-edited assumptions.

## 6. Risks the PRD must name

1. **Liability for wrong outputs** — a wrong FSI answer costs crores, and *ICC v. UpCodes* shows even marketing copy is actionable. Mitigation: pre-scrutiny positioning, clause citations with confidence, coverage maps, professional-verification disclaimer on every output, plus a verified-by-licensed-consultant premium tier (which doubles as the channel strategy).
2. **Regulatory data doesn't exist machine-readably** — bylaws are city-specific, PDF-native, bilingual, amended by circulars. Mitigation: per-city data-ops as a costed first-class workstream; sequence by regulatory consolidation (UDCPR); freshness SLA.
3. **Licensure boundary** — only registered architects/engineers may prepare and sign submitted plans (Architects Act 1972; *CoA v. Mukesh Goyal* confirms the software itself is lawful — say so in a regulatory-strategy section). Mitigation: "draft, not submittable" positioning; architect-of-record workflow; partner with architects rather than replacing them.
4. **Competition** — Zoneity live in six Indian cities; SoftTech owns the government seat in 500+ ULBs; global tools own the demo aesthetic; Autodesk could localize. Mitigation: corridor depth + residual-value output none of them has; PreDCR-compatible exports; partnership posture toward SoftTech.
5. **Trust destruction via overreach** — Delve (Google-backed generative urban design) shut down in 2026; confident fiction in one module poisons the modules that work. Mitigation: deterministic engine, narrow rule scope, honest coverage disclosure, design-partner accuracy benchmarks published before scaling, no "full plan in minutes" absolutes in marketing.

## 7. Success metrics for the MVP

1. **Time-to-feasibility-report** < 5–10 min from plot input (vs the 2–6-week consultant baseline).
2. ≥ 10 design-partner accounts running ≥ 3 real parcels each within 90 days.
3. **Accuracy audit:** ≥ 95% agreement with a licensed DTCP consultant on a 50-parcel validation set, published; zero tolerated FSI-envelope errors in production geographies.
4. **Rule coverage:** % of envelope-determining Gurugram DCR clauses machine-checked (honest target given the ~50% auto-checkability ceiling) + rule freshness (days from amendment to encoded).
5. **First-pass sanction-rate uplift** (or resubmission-count reduction) for plans pre-checked by UrbanOS vs baseline.
6. ≥ 30% of generated reports shared externally, and repeat purchase (second parcel within 90 days) per account — the trust/viral loop.
7. Paid conversion: free-snapshot → paid-report rate, with 3+ paid conversions (≥ ₹25k) or 1 annual contract within 6 months; a ₹-defined corridor revenue milestone before opening city three.

## Rejected recommendations (and why)

- **"Maharashtra UDCPR first"** (competitive lens) — rejected for Gurugram/NCR-first (matches the PRD's own Dwarka narrative and CREDAI NCR access); UDCPR becomes geography two, keeping its one-rulebook leverage.
- **"Launch 4 cities at once"** (business lens) — rejected: the compliance moat compounds per-city, and a shallow multi-city checker fails the accuracy metric that GTM depends on. (Demo data for 4 cities is fine; *sold* rule packs need curation.)
- **"Drop consultancies, developers only"** (wedge lens) — rejected: consultancies are the volume users and the distribution channel; a second pricing tier, not a second product.
- **"LLM-assisted rule checking in MVP"** (risk-lens tolerance) — rejected for a fully deterministic engine; LLMs only for intake parsing and narrative. The wrong-FSI horror story outweighs extraction convenience.
- **"Delay monetization until accuracy is proven"** — softened: charge design partners discounted per-report fees from day one; free pilots teach nothing about willingness-to-pay.
- **Seat-SaaS as the primary model** — rejected: per-verdict pricing leads; seats are secondary for consultancies.

# Appendix A — Competitive landscape (research brief)

## Competitor table

| Product | What it does | Who pays | Pricing (public) | What it does NOT do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Autodesk Forma** (ex-Spacemaker, acquired $240M 2020) | Cloud early-stage site design: massing options, sun/wind/noise/daylight analysis, area metrics | Architects, developers (EU/US) | ~$185/user/mo (annual) | No Indian bylaws/FAR regimes, no construction costs in ₹, no regulatory checking anywhere |
| **TestFit** | Real-time generative building configurator: multifamily/industrial unit counts, parking solvers, deal feasibility | US developers, architects, GCs | ~$12k–20k/yr/seat tiers | US-centric zoning assumptions; no compliance engine; no India presence |
| **ArchiStar** | AI site assessment + generative design + planning rules for AU/NZ parcels | AU developers, councils | Subscription, undisclosed (~AU$ thousands/yr) | Planning-rule coverage is Australia-only; proves the "codified planning rules" model works |
| **Giraffe** | Collaborative map-based urban design canvas w/ live metrics, app ecosystem | Urban designers, developers | Freemium + enterprise | Light on financial feasibility; no code compliance |
| **Deepblocks** | US parcel + zoning data → instant development capacity & pro-forma | US developers/investors | ~$99–500/mo | Entirely dependent on US parcel/zoning data availability |
| **Digital Blue Foam** | Generative urban design sandbox, sustainability scoring; Singapore-based, has done India pilots | Architects, corporates | Undisclosed SaaS | Design-exploration flavor; not a feasibility/compliance decision tool |
| **SoftTech AutoDCR/PreDCR** | Automated development-control-rules scrutiny of submitted CAD drawings; deployed in 500+ Indian ULBs incl. PMC, MCGM | **Government/ULBs** (authority-side) | Govt contracts | Serves the approval authority AFTER drawings exist; PreDCR checks architect drawings pre-submission; does NOT generate options, feasibility, or serve the developer's acquisition decision |
| **India adjacent** (Foyr, SmartVizs, proptech listing portals) | Interior/viz or discovery | — | — | Nobody does bylaw-aware development feasibility for Indian parcels |

## Whitespace insights

1. **The India compliance+feasibility combination is unowned.** Forma/TestFit have the generative tech but will not encode DTCP Haryana bylaws; AutoDCR has the bylaw logic but serves municipalities scrutinizing finished drawings. The pre-acquisition developer decision sits exactly between them.
2. **AutoDCR is proof-of-feasibility, not competition** — it demonstrates Indian DCRs CAN be codified at scale (500+ ULBs), de-risking UrbanOS's hardest technical claim.
3. **Every western tool monetizes the *design* moment; the Indian pain is the *underwriting* moment** — land is the largest capital outlay and FAR/TOD/purchasable-FAR math determines land value; nobody computes it for the buyer.
4. **Data-poor environment is a feature**: US tools (Deepblocks) are thin wrappers over rich public data. In India the data must be *built* — painful, but that's the moat once built.
5. **The consultant PDF is the incumbent** — the real competitor is a ₹5–25 lakh, 4–12 week manual feasibility cycle, which is also the pricing anchor.

## Differentiation recommendations

1. Lead with **bylaw-checked feasibility in minutes** (not "AI masterplanning" — Forma owns that story globally).
2. Build **named, versioned city rule-packs** with clause citations as the visible product asset.
3. Anchor pricing against the **consultant cycle (₹ lakhs/deal)**, not against SaaS seats.
4. Own the **report-that-gets-shared** (landowner/investor/lender audiences) as the growth loop.
5. Do NOT chase 3D/walkthroughs — that's incumbent turf and decision-irrelevant at the wedge moment.

# Appendix B — Wedge & MVP scope (research brief)

## Recommended wedge
UrbanOS should launch as the **pre-acquisition feasibility engine for mid-size Indian developers and their consultants**, starting with Gurugram/NCR. The workflow it replaces: the ₹5–25 lakh, 4–12 week pre-feasibility cycle (concept architect + DCR consultant + QS) that precedes every serious land deal — and which developers run on 5–10 parcels for every 1 they buy. The 10x moment: plot pin → 3 bylaw-checked concepts with FAR utilization, costs, revenue, ROI and a shareable PDF in under 10 minutes. TestFit's history validates starting from "the number the deal lives on" (unit count/yield) rather than from design beauty; Spacemaker validates selling optioning to developers, then expanding.

## Cut list (with reasons)
- **3D concepts & visual walkthroughs** — decision-irrelevant at pre-feasibility; massive engineering; Forma's turf.
- **Construction Intelligence** (phases/manpower/contractor scopes) — post-acquisition buyer stage; keep only an indicative timeline line in the report.
- **Sustainability Engine** — Phase-2 government/ESG story; keep a "Green" scenario archetype + green-area compliance checks.
- **Homeowner/house persona** — different product, different channel, ₹0 WTP for reports; use only as demo sugar.
- **Governments/smart-city agencies** — 18-month procurement; Phase 2+.

## Missing capabilities (ranked by impact)
1. **Map-based site ingestion** (draw boundary / KML/DXF upload / khasra lookup) — real deals start from parcels, not typed areas.
2. **Editable assumptions** (₹/sqft construction & sale rates, FAR premiums) — every developer overrides defaults; locked numbers = toy.
3. **Bylaw rule-pack depth for ONE city** (Gurugram DTCP: TOD bonus, purchasable FAR, stilt+4, EWS, parking ECS) with clause citations & versioning.
4. **Saved projects + scenario comparison view** — decisions are comparisons.
5. **Shareable branded report link** (not just PDF) — the distribution loop.
6. **Sensitivity that matches deal logic** (land price is the variable that kills deals — add land-cost input & sensitivity on it).
7. Consultant workspace (multi-client) — fast follow, not MVP.

## Landing-page sentence
**"Upload your plot. Get three bylaw-compliant development concepts with full financials — in 10 minutes, free of the ₹10-lakh feasibility cycle."**

# Appendix C — Business model & GTM (research brief)

## How the buyers buy today
- Indian developers: ~40k+ active, but the serviceable core is CREDAI's ~13,000 member developers across 230+ city chapters; mid-size NCR developers run lean teams and outsource feasibility per-deal to architects/DCR consultants (₹5–25 lakh, 4–12 weeks).
- Architects/planning consultancies: ~130k CoA-registered architects; DTCP Haryana maintains lists of licensed consultants who process change-of-land-use and licence applications — a mappable, finite channel.
- Software buying: developers buy little SaaS (ERP like Farvision/SAP at the large end); architects buy AutoCAD/Revit seats. The purchase trigger is deal-linked spend, not seats — which favors per-report entry pricing.

## Who pays first (ranked by WTP × accessibility)
1. **Mid-size developers (₹50–500 Cr GDV)** — direct pain (they fund feasibility), fast decision (promoter-led), clear ROI story vs consultant fees.
2. **Planning consultancies/architects serving them** — lower per-unit WTP but 10–50 feasibilities/yr; they become the channel and the accuracy validators.
3. **Land aggregators/brokers (top end)** — will pay per-report to dress deals; volatile, don't build for them.
4. **NBFC/HFC land-lending desks** — high WTP for underwriting standardization; longer cycle — year-2 expansion.
5. Governments — Phase 2+; procurement cycles kill startups.

## Pricing models (₹, concrete)
- **A. Per-report (land the account):** ₹25,000–₹1,00,000 per parcel feasibility (vs ₹5–25L consultant cycle). Objection-free trial economics; caps at deal flow.
- **B. Seat SaaS (expand):** Developer plan ₹3–6L/yr (3 seats, unlimited parcels, comparison, branded reports); Consultancy plan ₹1.5–3L/yr multi-client. Benchmarks: Forma ~$185/seat/mo, TestFit ~$12–20k/yr — India WTP sits at the low band but per-deal anchoring beats seat anchoring.
- **C. %-of-deal success fee** — rejected: unverifiable, channel conflict, delays revenue.
Recommended: A → B ladder; enterprise/API (lenders, aggregators) in year 2.

## First GTM motion
- **Geography: Gurugram/NCR only** (highest land values, single DTCP regime, the PRD's own example).
- 10 design-partner developers at discounted per-report pricing via **CREDAI NCR chapter + the DTCP-licensed consultant list**; run side-by-side vs their consultant feasibility on live parcels.
- **Free "Gurugram FAR / TOD calculator"** as a top-of-funnel lead magnet (SEO + broker WhatsApp forwards).
- The **shareable branded report** is the referral engine — every report lands in front of a landowner, an investor, a lender.
- Partnerships, not replacement, with 3–5 marquee DCR consultancies (their letterhead + UrbanOS engine = trust transfer).

## Moat to build from day one
1. **Versioned bylaw rule-packs** (curation pipeline + change monitoring — compounding, per-city).
2. **Anonymized feasibility benchmark data** (₹/sqft cost & price actuals by micro-market from user-edited assumptions — a network effect nobody in India has).
3. Consultant-validated accuracy record (published) — the trust asset that gates all referral growth.

# Appendix D — Risk & reality check (research brief)

## 1. Overpromised claims in the PRD
- **"Regulatory compliance checks ... automatically" (all bylaws/policies)** — Indian DCRs are PDF-bound, amended by circulars, and internally contradictory across GO/master-plan/bylaw layers. AutoDCR took years per-state with government cooperation to codify scrutiny rules. Claiming automatic compliance across jurisdictions at MVP is not credible; claim *rule-pack-scoped* checks with citations instead.
- **"Construction plans, manpower requirements ... within minutes"** — parametric estimates are fine, but "construction plans" implies executable programmes; a contractor's programme depends on soil, procurement, monsoon. Rename to "indicative execution roadmap".
- **"Projects of any scale" / all seven personas** — the homeowner-to-government span is a focus killer and each persona has a different legal exposure profile.
- **3D walkthroughs "within minutes"** — technically feasible only as generic massing; adds no decision value; drop from MVP.

## 2. The liability/trust problem (researched precedents)
- **AutoDCR sits authority-side**: the municipality runs it, the architect certifies the drawings, and legal responsibility stays with the licensed professional + sanctioning authority. It never tells a developer "this is compliant" as a service — this is exactly the liability UrbanOS would be taking on if it's careless.
- **Licensure boundary (Architects Act 1972 / CoA):** only registered architects (and licensed engineers/supervisors per local building bylaws) may prepare and sign plans submitted for sanction. UrbanOS outputs must be positioned as *pre-design decision support*, never "sanctionable plans".
- Mitigations that must be IN the PRD: (a) every compliance verdict carries the rule-pack version + clause citation + "verify with licensed professional" line; (b) confidence levels per check; (c) an audit log of rule versions used per report; (d) E&O-style legal review before charging for compliance outputs.

## 3. Data-acquisition reality
- Bylaws/master plans: PDFs on DTCP/ULB sites; no APIs; amendments via circulars — needs a **human-curated rule-pack pipeline with change monitoring** (cost this as COGS).
- Parcel geometry: khasra/CLU records partially digitized (state-varying); MVP should accept drawn/uploaded boundaries and NOT promise cadastral lookup.
- Market rates: circle rates published per district (PDF); real transaction prices opaque — make all rates user-editable and label sources.
- Costs: CPWD DSR/market thumb-rules exist and are defensible for indicative estimates.

## 4. What the PRD must ADD to be credible
1. **Non-goals section**: no plan sanction, no construction drawings, no cadastral truth, no price prediction (v1).
2. **Human-in-the-loop positioning statement** as a product principle, not a disclaimer footnote.
3. **Accuracy metric & validation plan** (agreement % vs licensed consultants on a benchmark parcel set, published).
4. **Rule-pack scope statement per release** ("Gurugram DTCP residential/group-housing/commercial, vN, dated").
5. **Legal review milestone** before monetizing compliance outputs.
6. **Data strategy section** (curation pipeline, change monitoring, sourcing labels).

Bottom line: the deterministic-engine + citations + human-verification framing is not a weaker product — it is the only version institutions can trust, and trust is the product.
